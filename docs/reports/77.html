Executive Summary

Today's corpus is anomalous: 57% of papers score high-novelty and 97.5% bridge multiple domains, signaling a genuine convergence moment rather than routine output. The dominant signal is formal verification meeting machine learning—AI systems are now constructing and checking proofs of landmark mathematics, while simultaneously the limits of that process are being rigorously exposed. In parallel, physics-constrained generative models are penetrating chemistry, materials science, neuroscience, and robotics at scale.

Key Findings

  • AI-assisted formalization reaches landmark mathematics. An AI-Assisted Formalization of the Poincaré Conjecture demonstrates that multi-agent LLM workflows operating against proof blueprints can now formally verify one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems in Lean 4. The reusable infrastructure produced is as significant as the result itself.

  • But autoformalisation has a hard semantic ceiling. Navier-Stokes lost in translation proves—using the Solvability Complexity Index hierarchy—that Lean verification of an AI-translated proof does not guarantee the underlying natural language argument is correct. This directly challenges the epistemic status of OpenAI's concurrent Navier-Stokes announcement and demands a two-layer review standard for AI-generated mathematics.

  • Physics-constrained generative architectures are converging across domains. Neural Petri flows for chemical reactions enforces Petri net semantics (valence conservation) for every weight configuration—not just at training optimum—unifying atom mapping, reaction classification, and product prediction. Simultaneously, OxiGen enforces charge neutrality in crystal generation via exact finite-state automaton inference. Both represent a structural shift from soft penalty constraints to hard architectural guarantees.

  • Molecular discovery complexity collapses from O(d) to O(log d). Information-Dense Synthesis for Molecular Discovery uses pooled mixture testing with information-maximizing synthesis to achieve this reduction in experimental complexity for sparse chemical spaces where standard Bayesian optimization offers negligible gain over random search. This reframes the drug discovery experimental loop at a fundamental level.

  • Optimal planning achieves machine-checked admissibility guarantees. LeanPlan is the first system to machine-verify that LLM-generated heuristics are admissible in Lean 4 before using them, enabling provably optimal planning and outperforming state-of-the-art satisficing planners. This closes the longstanding gap between LLM heuristic quality and formal optimality guarantees.

Emerging Themes

Three overlapping waves are visible in today's data. First, formal verification is becoming a runtime component of AI systems, not a post-hoc audit tool—seen in LeanPlan, the Poincaré formalization, and implicitly critiqued by the Navier-Stokes translation paper. This creates an urgent sub-problem: verification confirms syntactic correctness, but semantic faithfulness to intended mathematics remains unresolved. Second, hard structural constraints are replacing soft losses across generative modeling: Petri net firing semantics in chemistry, charge neutrality via FSA in materials, cylindrical geodesics for physiological waveforms (Cylindrical Geodesic Flow Matching), and sensor-topology Matérn priors in EEG generation (Sensor Geometry as a Flow-Matching Prior). The pattern is domain-specific physics baked into architecture, not trained away. Third, multi-modal sensing and action are unifying: Sensor-Language-Action Models spanning 116,000 individuals and 79 sensor types, and QF3 achieving first-ever zero-shot sim-to-hardware transfer for humanoid locomotion via off-policy flow RL, both reflect a maturation of foundation model thinking beyond vision-language into physical and physiological sensing. The cross-cutting signal: constraint satisfaction and physical grounding are becoming the primary differentiators between toy and deployable AI systems.
